## 2. Thesis subject and conceptual framework

The thesis compares how in-house and outsourced operating arrangements affect an organisation’s operational and strategic/business risk profile. Hybrid arrangements are included because real operating activities may combine internal and external delivery.

The comparison is not simply “which model is better.” It examines how operating model changes:
- Resource configuration.
- Distribution of control.
- Internal and external dependencies.
- Risk exposure.
- Consequences if the activity fails.
- Detection, response and recovery capability.
- Residual risk after controls.

Working analytical chain:

**Operating model → Resource configuration → Control distribution → Dependency structure → Risk exposure → Consequences → Response/recovery → Residual risk.**

Use this chain consistently across the knowledge base, questionnaire, analysis and discussion.

**Central research question:** How do in-house and outsourced operations affect a company’s risk profile?

Earlier planning developed subquestions around:
1. Operational and strategic/business risks associated with in-house operations.
2. Operational and strategic/business risks associated with outsourced operations.
3. Differences in control distribution.
4. Effects of internal resource and external provider dependencies.
5. Effects on continuity, resilience, response and recovery.
6. Accountability, monitoring and governance.
7. Make-or-buy decision factors.
8. Respondents’ overall assessment of the current arrangement’s effect on risk.

Refine exact wording against the latest approved thesis plan. Do not silently expand the number of research questions.

**Scope discipline**
- Questionnaire-only data collection is the current decision. Do not add interviews or another data-collection method without explicit researcher and any required supervisor approval.
- Respondents assess one operating activity they know sufficiently well. A company may use different models for different activities; do not assume the entire company has one operating model.
- The study is exploratory and comparative. A cross-sectional questionnaire does not prove causation.
- Do not describe a small purposive sample as statistically representative of all European companies.
