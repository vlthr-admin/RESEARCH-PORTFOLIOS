## 1. Executive Summary

This report documents the descriptive analysis of 30 questionnaire respondent records. The current grouping is predominantly in-house (n = 9), predominantly outsourced (n = 12) and hybrid (n = 9). The findings describe assessments reported by these respondents and should not be generalised to the wider population.

The main descriptive patterns concern internal skill dependency, external provider dependency, hybrid responsibility clarity, common problem-detection and recovery ratings, reported failure consequences, and make-or-buy decision factors. The dataset is cross-sectional and based on self-reported assessments. It does not establish causal effects or objective incident rates. The additional-insight open-text field is supplementary; qualitative findings should be included only if usable text responses are present and a documented coding process is completed.

## 4. Cross-Model Comparison: Resilience, Governance & Detection (RQ3, RQ4)

The questionnaire evaluated three universal resilience and governance metrics across all respondents regardless of operating model:

| Operating Model | Group Size ($n$) | Problem Detection (D1)<br/>Mean (Median) [SD] | Recovery Effectiveness (D2)<br/>Mean (Median) [SD] | Accountability Clarity (D3)<br/>Mean (Median) [SD] |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Predominantly In-House** | $n = 9$ | **7.33** (7.00) [0.50] | **5.67** (6.00) [0.50] | **8.00** (8.00) [0.71] |
| **Predominantly Outsourced**| $n = 12$ | **5.50** (5.50) [0.52] | **5.67** (5.50) [0.78] | **7.33** (7.50) [0.98] |
| **Combination (Hybrid)** | $n = 9$ | **5.56** (6.00) [0.53] | **6.22** (7.00) [0.97] | **6.44** (6.00) [0.53] |

### Comparative Observations:
1. **Detection Advantage for In-House:** In-house operations hold a 1.83-point lead over outsourcing in early problem detection ($7.33$ vs. $5.50$). Without third-party communication layers, internal operational deviations are identified faster.
2. **Recovery Advantage for Hybrid:** Hybrid operations hold the highest recovery effectiveness ($6.22$ vs. $5.67$). When disruptions occur, having both internal staff and external supplier options offers dual fallback mechanisms.
3. **Governance Gap in Hybrid:** In-house has the highest accountability clarity ($8.00$), followed by outsourcing ($7.33$), while hybrid ranks lowest ($6.44$). Contracts clarify outsourced duties, but hybrid models suffer from "grey zones" between internal staff and external contractors.

### Core Conclusion for Chapter 5 & 6:
Nearly three-quarters (73.3%) of respondents corroborate that operating model decisions do not eliminate risk, but rather **transform and redistribute it**:
- Moving to outsourcing reduces internal capacity/capital burdens while elevating dependency and monitoring exposures.
- Retaining activities in-house maximizes control and problem detection while intensifying single-point skill and fixed capacity risks.
- Combining both in hybrid models delivers recovery flexibility but introduces coordination friction and blurred accountability.
